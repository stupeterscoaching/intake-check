# Output shape

Every flag the system produces has the same four parts, in this order.

1. **What the client said**, quoted or closely paraphrased, with the source.
2. **What is on file**, with a citation for each fact — file and line.
3. **The statement that they disagree.** Plain, no hedging.
4. **A question**, addressed to a person.

## Worked example

> **Length of service**
>
> Client stated 4 years, start date March 2022 (`intake-form.md:9`, `:10`).
>
> On file: an employment agreement dated 4 July 2016 commencing 18 July 2016
> (`docs/employment-contract-2016.md:9`), a second dated 28 February 2022
> commencing 14 March 2022 (`docs/employment-contract-2022.md:9`), and an ROE
> noting a prior ROE was issued in 2021 (`docs/record-of-employment.md:14`).
>
> These do not agree. Does the break between 2021 and 2022 affect continuity of
> service?

## The never-answer rule

The output ends at the question. It never contains:

- whether a discrepancy changes the case
- whether continuity, notice or entitlement applies
- whether there is a case
- what anything is worth
- what the client should do
- a theory connecting two facts

Stating that a conduct warning exists and that the stated reason for termination
was restructuring is correct. Suggesting the first explains the second is not,
**however obvious the connection looks.** Whether they are connected is the
entire question, and it is not ours.

## Tone

Written for a person who will act on it in the next hour. No preamble, no
summary of what the system did, no confidence scores. If there are no
discrepancies, say so in one line.
