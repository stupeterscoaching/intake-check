# Document types

What each type is, and what to read from it. Jurisdiction: Ontario.

| Type | Read from it | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Employment agreement** | Position, commencement date, compensation, bonus terms, notice terms, prior-service clauses | Multiple agreements over time. A later one may or may not supersede. Both get recorded. |
| **Offer letter** | Same fields, pre-signature | Terms often change between offer and agreement |
| **Job description** | Title, reporting line, direct reports, authority | Dated separately from the contract; may describe a different version of the role |
| **Pay statement** | Rate, period, gross, year to date, bonus paid | The most reliable record of what was *actually* paid, as opposed to agreed |
| **Record of Employment (ROE)** | First day worked, last day paid, reason code, insurable hours | Notes about prior ROEs are a service-history signal |
| **Termination letter** | End date, stated reason, notice or pay in lieu, vacation pay | The stated reason is a fact about the letter, not about the world |
| **Written warning** | Date, subject, severity, acknowledgement | Conduct documents sit awkwardly beside non-conduct termination reasons. Record both. Connect neither. |

## Prior-service clauses

An agreement may recognise earlier service **for a limited purpose** — vacation
entitlement, for example. Record exactly what the clause says and what it is
limited to. Do not generalise it to other entitlements.

## What never gets extracted

- Anything requiring a legal test to produce. Continuity of service, whether
  notice was adequate, whether a term is enforceable.
- Anything derived by arithmetic that the document does not state. If a pay
  stub shows a two-week gross, that is the fact. An annualised figure is not.
