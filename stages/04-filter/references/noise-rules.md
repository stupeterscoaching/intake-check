# What is not a discrepancy

A system that always returns flags is worse than no system, because the reader
stops reading. Most of this file is about restraint.

## Approximation

If the client hedged, the hedge is the specification.

| Claim | Fact | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "About $55,000" | $54,800 | **Approximation.** "About" was doing exactly its job. |
| "$72,000" | $64,000 base | **Real.** Stated plainly, and the gap is large. |
| "Just over 2 years" | 2 years 4 months | **Approximation.** |
| "4 years" | Contracts from 2016 and 2022 | **Real.** Not an approximation of anything. |

Rule of thumb: a hedged claim within a few percent of the fact is an
approximation. ?? The threshold is a judgment call and should be set by a
receiver, not by us.

### Call notes round

`intake-notes.md` is admin's write-up of a conversation, not the client's own
words on paper. Where the notes give a rounded figure and `intake-form.md`
states the same quantity exactly, the notes figure is an **approximation even
with no hedge word** — provided it is what the exact figure rounds to:

| Quantity | Rounds to the nearest |
|---|---|
| Length of service | half year |
| Salary or annual pay | $1,000 |
| Dates | month |

"Four and a half years" in the notes against "4 years 8 months" on the form is
approximation: 4 years 8 months rounds to 4½. "Three years" against "4 years 8
months" is not, and stays `client inconsistent`. The form's exact figure is
still compared against the documents on its own row.

## Equivalence

Different words, same thing.

- "Manager" against "Catering Manager" — the same role, informally named.
- "Spring 2023" against 17 April 2023 — April is spring.
- "End of August" against 29 August — consistent.

But: "Operations Manager" against "Operations **Supervisor**" is **real**. The
job description shows no direct reports and no hiring or budget authority. That
is a difference in seniority, not in phrasing.

   practitioner's view.

## Arithmetic is not evidence

Never keep a difference produced by calculating something the documents do not
state.

A pay statement showing $31,420 year to date in mid-June, against a claim of
"around $78,000", is **not** a discrepancy. It is arithmetic on a partial year
of variable earnings. The correct outcome is `unverifiable`, with a note that
full payroll history would settle it.

## Disclosed is not discrepant

If the client volunteered something and a document confirms it, that is
agreement. A client who says "I had one warning in 2023" and has one warning
from 2023 on file has been consistent.

## Unmentioned is not a discrepancy

A fact on file that the client never raised has nothing to disagree with. Stage
03 lists these so the comparison is complete in both directions; stage 04
**discards every one.** A termination letter alone carries dozens of them.

Discard them as one row in the discarded table: outcome `unmentioned`, the
fields listed, reason "on file, not raised by the client — nothing to compare
against". Never promote one to a flag, however significant it looks. Deciding
that an unraised fact matters is a judgment about the case.

## A claim that something never happened

"Never raised again", "no other warnings", "nothing else was said" — a document
can contradict a claim like this but no document can prove it. If a document on
file contradicts it, stage 03 has already marked it `differs` and it is judged
like any other difference. If nothing on file contradicts it, **discard it**:
outcome `absence claim`, reason "claims something did not happen; nothing on
file contradicts it". It does not go on the page as unverifiable — asking for
paperwork that would prove a negative is noise.

This applies only to claims that something did **not** happen. A positive claim
with no document behind it ("I got a $5,000 bonus in 2023") stays unverifiable.

## Unverifiable is its own outcome

Cannot-check is neither a flag nor a discard. It goes on the page in its own
section with the document that would settle it named.
