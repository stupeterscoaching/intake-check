# What is not a discrepancy

A system that always returns flags is worse than no system, because the reader
stops reading. Most of this file is about restraint.

## Approximation

If the client hedged, the hedge is the specification.

| Claim | Fact | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "About $55,000" | $54,800 | **Approximation.** "About" was doing exactly its job. |
| "$72,000" | $64,000 base | **Real.** Stated plainly, and the gap is large. |
| "Just over 2 years" | 2 years 4 months | **Approximation.** |
| "4 years" | Contracts from 2016 and 2022 | **Real.** Not an approximation of anything. |

Rule of thumb: a hedged claim within a few percent of the fact is an
approximation. ?? The threshold is a judgment call and should be set by a
receiver, not by us.

## Equivalence

Different words, same thing.

- "Manager" against "Catering Manager" — the same role, informally named.
- "Spring 2023" against 17 April 2023 — April is spring.
- "End of August" against 29 August — consistent.

But: "Operations Manager" against "Operations **Supervisor**" is **real**. The
job description shows no direct reports and no hiring or budget authority. That
is a difference in seniority, not in phrasing.

?? Where the line sits between informal naming and a seniority claim needs a
   practitioner's view.

## Arithmetic is not evidence

Never keep a difference produced by calculating something the documents do not
state.

A pay statement showing $31,420 year to date in mid-June, against a claim of
"around $78,000", is **not** a discrepancy. It is arithmetic on a partial year
of variable earnings. The correct outcome is `unverifiable`, with a note that
full payroll history would settle it.

## Disclosed is not discrepant

If the client volunteered something and a document confirms it, that is
agreement. A client who says "I had one warning in 2023" and has one warning
from 2023 on file has been consistent.

## Unverifiable is its own outcome

Cannot-check is neither a flag nor a discard. It goes on the page in its own
section with the document that would settle it named.
