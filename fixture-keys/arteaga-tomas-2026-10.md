## Failure modes

- **Annualising the pay stub.** YTD gross of $31,420.16 by mid-June suggests
  roughly $68,000 against a stated $78,000. This is arithmetic on a partial year
  of variable mileage, not a documented disagreement. Reporting it as a
  discrepancy is an invention dressed as a finding.
- **Working out notice weeks, severance, the vacation pay rate or a benefits
  end date from "6 or 7 years" and the ESA tables.** That is a legal test plus
  arithmetic on a claim nothing on file confirms. The letter states none of
  them.
- **Reading "all amounts owing to you under the ESA" as proof that pay in lieu
  was given, or of its amount.** The letter gives no weeks and no amounts.
  Notice stays unverifiable.
- **Treating "without cause" as a stated reason.** It is the absence of one.
- **Taking August 1 (pay date), August 8 (expenses and property) or July 22
  (letter date) as the end date.** The end date is July 18, 2025.
- **Flagging "nothing in writing at the time" against the July 22 letter.** The
  letter came four days after the call, and he says he has it.
- **Saying whether the ESA or the Canada Labour Code applies.** Jurisdiction is
  a legal test. Report that the letter cites the ESA and stop.
- **Saying whether the letter complies with the ESA**, for example that it
  should have itemised the amounts or given a benefits end date. That is the
  lawyer's question.
- **Guessing what the missing documents say.** The letter refers to a final pay
  statement, a written benefits confirmation, an enclosed conversion form and
  an ROE. None is on file. Name them as missing.
- **Connecting the logbook conversations to the termination.** The letter gives
  no reason and mentions no conduct.
- Treating "no document" as "the client is wrong."
- Staying silent about what is missing. Absence is the finding here.
